# Notebook Authoring Rules

## Core Rule

The notebook is a physical artifact first.

Pages do not stretch.  
Pages do not clip meaningful content.  
Pages do not auto-fit story material.

If content does not fit, it moves to another page.

## Page Geometry

All notebook pages use fixed physical dimensions in the reader.

Focus mode zooms the view of the same page.  
It must not resize or reshape the page.

## Spread Composition

Each spread should have a declared emotional function.

Examples:
- establish care
- introduce contradiction
- preserve absence
- recover damaged material
- destabilize interpretation

No spread should exist only to hold content.

## Pagination Rule

Every meaningful artifact belongs to an authored page definition.

Avoid component-level overflow decisions.  
Use page definitions to determine what appears where.

## Reveal Cadence

Reveals should feel recovered through handling, not unlocked by a game system.

Preferred reveal modes:
- delayed residue
- pressure marks
- faint graphite
- folded notes
- provenance mismatch
- remembered wording drift

Avoid:
- loud glitching
- obvious horror effects
- excessive UI explanation

## Restraint Doctrine

The notebook is strongest when it is quiet.

A single emotionally precise line is stronger than a full explanation.

Preserve ambiguity whenever possible.

## Eli Chain Lesson

The Eli chain works because it preserves competing truths:

- Eli may have been changing.
- The group may have changed him by documenting him as already changed.

Do not resolve this tension too cleanly.

## Sacred Artifact Realism

Every mark should imply use, care, argument, recovery, or preservation.

Nothing should feel decorative only.
